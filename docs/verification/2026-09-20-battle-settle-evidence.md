## What this proves, and what it does not

One disposable world. Three readers: the attacker **P1** on the Roblox door (`/api/roblox/session`, `/state`, `/commands`), the defender **D** and a watcher **W** on the web door (`/api/auth/register`, `/api/world/snapshot`, `/api/world/commands`, `/api/world/events`, `/api/world/stream`). The SQLite file read directly for what the API hides or does not carry (the frozen battle inputs, the plan rows, the Freehold's notifications, war points).

Three attacks from P1, two targets:

| | Army | Plan | Target | Attended? | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | 5 Squires | best plan at **launch** | D's seeded capital (34 troops, wall 1) | no — auto-resolved | **DEFEAT**, total wipe |
| **B** | 10 Berserkers | none at launch; best plan at **`battle.open`** | Saltmarsh Freehold (10 Squires, wall 0, 400/350/250) | yes — opened, 5 orders, resolved | **VICTORY**, 2 prisoners, 90 wood |
| **C** | 5 Squires | none (`UNPLANNED`) | the same Freehold, arriving beside B | no — auto-resolved after B | **VICTORY** over the empty garrison B left, 77 wood + 48 stone |

**The oracle.** Every settled battle's stored `outcome_json` was recomputed by the drill with game-core's own `resolveBattle` from the battle row's frozen inputs — both armies, both troop-level sets, the wall, the stock, the plan, the order count and the seed — and had to match **byte for byte**. All three did. Loot was recomputed with `calculateLoot`, war points as `max(10, 3 × casualties)`, return legs with `marchDurationSeconds(distance, "return", homewardArmy)`, the seed as `sha256(worldId:marchId:openedAt)[:24]`, the grace as 180 s from acceptance. Refusal codes and messages are literals from a reading of `server/src/store.ts`. So this proves the server settles with **the shared rule and nothing else** — it does not judge whether the rule is good. Because the seed includes `opened_at`, two runs of "the same" battle are different battles (smoke run 4 had 10 Berserkers survive and 100 wood; the recorded run 9 and 90): the drill recomputes rather than hard-codes.

**The B8 mid-settle recipe, for the follow-up:** steps 1–5 of this drill leave a battle `open` with the attacker's `battle.resolve` one HTTP call away, and steps 3–8 leave two attacks whose `auto_resolve_at` is known to the millisecond. Five observations recorded and **not judged**: (1) **an unattended battle's stamps are catch-up time, not the deadline** — `opened_at`/`resolved_at` are 57–236 ms after `auto_resolve_at` (the interval), whereas a scout report is stamped at its Spy's `arrivesAt` (marches drill); the two stamps are separate `now()` reads and differ by 0–1 ms, and the `battle.open` grace is likewise `now() + 180 s` read a millisecond after the session's `opened_at` (the first recorded run failed 3/10 on my "exactly `opened_at + 180 s`" oracle — the server's, not the rule's, millisecond). (2) **A total wipe still walks home**: A's `return` march carries `{}`, takes the full 37 s leg at the reference pace, and lands as "0 troops returned to Battle One's Realm Keep." (3) **Two plan records per attack**: after `battle.open`, the session holds the plan given at open (fought) while `local_march_plans.plan_json` keeps the launch plan (`UNPLANNED` for B); harmless today because settlement reads the session, but two records for one fact. (4) **A Freehold kingdom accumulates notifications nobody can read** (`freehold-3` has two `battle` rows) — B9 retention territory. (5) Still true from the marches drill: **nothing tells a defender an army is at its walls** before it is fought; the first the defender hears is `battle.started` + `battle.resolved` in the same instant.
